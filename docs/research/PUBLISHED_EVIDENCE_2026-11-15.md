# World models should anchor Jack's brain

Still unpublished: doing it with a world model, without a word-label loss, in one life, as advice. (2) Dynalang is 148–281M parameters and took 3.75 A100 GPU-days for 50M steps; the ~10M figure belongs to its model-free baselines. (3) The SoundSpaces figures quoted are SPL (success weighted by path length), not success rate. (4) "No published agent keeps a memory across episodes except through its weights or replay" is too strong: episodic-control value tables, carried hidden state in meta-learning, and episodic stores recalled several episodes later exist; what is unpublished is one lasting world with death and rebirth at small scale. The published record points to one whole-brain design for Jack: a **Dreamer-style recurrent world model**. It is the only family with a single fixed configuration proven from scratch on **more than 150 tasks in 8 domains** ([DreamerV3, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08744-2)). It is also the only family that has learned on physical robots within **10⁴ to 10⁵ steps** while PPO, SAC and Rainbow failed on the same machines ([DayDreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14176)). And it has a published recipe for feeding images, body vectors, hunger-type needs and words into one shared state ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104); [Dynalang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01399)). The shortlist should hold only two other designs. One is a **replay-based model-free actor-critic with a small recurrent core**: the cheap, simple control, and the design with the most direct evidence on ray-sensed foraging. The other is a **decoder-free latent world model (TD-MPC2 lineage)**, kept for the later human-like body, where it leads on high-joint-count control ([TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)). The strongest argument against the recommendation is cost and simplicity. Dreamer spends far more computation per step, its headline results come from 12M–200M parameters, and nobody has published how its smallest preset performs. At equal steps, plain model-free agents matched it on a 3-D survival world ([Avalon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13417)). The team's race of seven learners could not have told any design apart, for three reasons. At **40,000 decisions** it stopped below every published foraging budget (about 30% of the fastest, 0.1% of the 3-D survival ones). With **2 seeds**, no ranking can be statistically valid. And its settings matched the published failure conditions: a 256-step replay, a 4-second horizon, and exploration noise that averages out. The fair-test recipe follows from the record. Use 10⁶ decisions per arm on the current rover. Pass every implementation through known-answer checks and a learnability test first. Fix the reward scale, horizon, exploration noise, replay and normalisation. Use 5–20 paired seeds sized by a pilot. Commit in writing to a predicted outcome before each run.

## How to read this report

This report is for the project's owner and for the agents who will act on it, so technical terms are explained when they first appear. The table below collects the ones that recur. Every number carries a link to its source. **[unverified]** marks claims the research notes could not confirm against a primary source. **(inference)** marks arithmetic or reasoning derived from published numbers rather than measured by anyone. An "environment step" or "decision" is one action by the creature; for Jack that is 0.2 s of life.

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Reinforcement learning (RL) | Learning to act by trial and error from a reward signal |
| World model | A network that learns to predict what the senses will report next, given an action; the brain can then "imagine" outcomes |
| Model-free | Learns which actions pay off directly, without predicting the world |
| Actor-critic | Two heads: the actor chooses actions; the critic estimates how good the current situation is |
| Replay buffer | A memory of past experience that the learner re-studies many times |
| Replay ratio | How many times, on average, stored experience is trained on for each new step lived |
| Discount (γ) and horizon | How much future reward counts; the horizon is roughly 1/(1−γ) decisions |
| Advantage normalisation | Rescaling the "better or worse than expected" signal before a policy update |
| GRU / LSTM | Small recurrent memory cells that carry information from step to step |
| Seed | One independent training run started from different random numbers |
| Plasticity loss | A network gradually losing its ability to learn new things |
| Curriculum | Starting with an easy version of the task and making it harder as skill grows |
| Parameters | The adjustable numbers (weights) inside a network; a rough measure of brain size |

## Three brain designs survive the evidence, and one should lead

### What the large published tests show, side by side

Only three families combine a broad, repeatable from-scratch record with a published configuration small enough for the project's hardware. The others fail one test or the other. Active-inference and predictive-coding brains have **no large benchmark result**; their own authors describe them as "restricted to low-dimensional and idealized situations" ([Tschantz et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10601)). Predictive coding as a learning rule matches ordinary backpropagation only up to about seven layers ([Pinchetti et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01163)). The most visible recent active-inference claim, AXIOM, was tested only on a 10,000-step benchmark of the authors' own design, with "engineered" object priors ([AXIOM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24784)). Transformer and diffusion world models top the Crafter survival benchmark at **58.1% at 10M steps** ([EMERALD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04075)) and **67–70% reward at 1M on Craftax-classic** ([Dedieu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01591)). But each 1M steps costs roughly 10–46 GPU-hours on high-end cards, and none has been tested on continuous bodies or many senses, so they belong on a watch list.

### Candidate A: a Dreamer-style world model

**How it works, in plain words.** Each sense passes through its own small encoder. Images go through a convolutional network; touch, needs and body readings go through small dense networks after a "symlog" squashing that tames large values. The encoder outputs are merged into one internal state that is carried forward by a GRU memory. The brain is trained mainly to **predict its own next sensations and rewards**. A separate actor and critic then learn by "dreaming": they roll that model forward 15 steps for many imagined futures and improve the policy on those ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). The ablation study in the same paper says performance "rests predominantly on the unsupervised objective of its world model". In other words, most of the learning comes from predicting the senses, not from the reward ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). That is exactly what Jack needs, because his reward is rare and his senses are rich.

**Track record at scale.** One configuration covered Atari (200M steps), ProcGen, DMLab (100M), proprioceptive control (500K) and visual control (1M) ([DreamerV3 arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). It was the first algorithm to collect **diamonds in Minecraft from scratch**, without human data. All 10 seeds found one within 100M steps, on one GPU for 9 days, although diamonds appeared in only 0.4% of episodes ([DreamerV3 arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). Its Minecraft input is the closest published match to Jack's: a first-person image, inventory vectors, and **health, hunger and breath scalars**, all in one model ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). On real hardware, the related DayDreamer agent taught a quadruped to **roll over, stand up and walk in 1 hour** with no simulator, and taught a robot arm sparse-reward pick-and-place in 8 hours. On those same robots, SAC learned only to roll over, and Rainbow and PPO learned only "short-sighted" grasp-and-drop ([DayDreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14176)). Converted to steps (inference), those runs are about 72,000 decisions (1 h at 20 Hz) and 57,600 decisions (8 h at 2 Hz): the same order as Jack's 40,000-decision race. The animal-cognition benchmark Animal-AI 3 makes the point in a 3-D foraging arena. With a curriculum, Dreamer placed **25th of 64** on the full testbed against PPO's 61st (a random agent placed 60th) ([Animal-AI 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11414)). On that arena's foraging task, Dreamer reached about **18 of 22 by 90k steps**, faster than PPO ([Animal-AI 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11414)).

**Strengths.** It is the only family whose published ablation shows a fused, predictive state using extra inputs where model-free agents get worse. Dynalang, built on DreamerV3, improved with language hints, while model-free IMPALA and R2D2 agents "see performance decrease with language hints" ([Dynalang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01399)). It scales smoothly. "Increasing the model size directly translates to both higher task performance and a lower data requirement", with all settings fixed from 12M to 400M ([DreamerV3, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08744-2)). That means the Mac mini and any future compute can buy a bigger brain without retuning. Replay comes built in, and replay is the main published defence against forgetting ([Kessler et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15944)). DayDreamer showed it adapting to pushes within 10 minutes and recovering from a drastic lighting change within hours ([DayDreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14176)).

**Weaknesses.** Memory over long spans is poor without fixes. On Memory Maze after 100M steps, Dreamer scored 4.1 on the largest maze against a human 67.7 ([Memory Maze](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13383)). Reconstructing whole images wastes capacity: "DreamerV3 fails to perceive crucial details" ([EMERALD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04075)). It is unstable on the 38-joint Dog body and slower than TD-MPC2 on Humanoid ([TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)). Its only exploration drive is a small fixed entropy bonus ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). The biggest weakness for this project is wall-clock cost. On Craftax-classic at 1M steps, a tuned PPO with a GRU reached 55.5% reward in **15 minutes** on an A100, while DreamerV3 took about **2,100 minutes** for 47–53% ([Dedieu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01591)). Searching a model at decision time (EfficientZero V2) beats DreamerV3 on 50 of 66 tasks when only 50k–200k steps are allowed ([EfficientZero V2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00564)).

**What it needs to work.** It needs the full "robustness kit", not a stripped-down version: symlog inputs, two-hot reward and value heads, KL balancing with free bits, return normalisation that only scales down, and 1% "unimix" ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). Three more parts are load-bearing and cannot be removed: dynamics that take the previous action as input, an actor and critic trained on imagined trajectories, and a large replay buffer (default 5×10⁶ steps) trained hundreds of times per step lived ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). The research notes conclude that a network without an action input and without imagination "is not Dreamer in any published sense" (inference from the published definition). It also needs a GPU. The proprioceptive benchmark took **0.3 A100-days for 500K steps** at 12M parameters ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). There is **no published throughput** for T4, P100, CPU or Apple silicon, so the cost on the project's free hardware is unknown until measured.

**Smallest published configuration.** The released code has a `size1m` preset (512 recurrent units, 64-unit layers, 4 classes per latent) used for proprioceptive control at replay ratio 1024. The paper reports **no scores** for it ([configs.yaml](https://github.com/danijar/dreamerv3/blob/main/dreamerv3/configs.yaml)). The paper's smallest reported sizes are 8M (v1) and 12M (v2). At 12M, Dreamer gave "the same performance" as 200M on the two control suites ([DreamerV3 arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). On Crafter, shrinking hurts more. The 37M model scored 34.9% against 39.6% for 200M at 10M frames ([EMERALD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04075)), and the 8M model plateaued at about half the return of the 200M model ([DreamerV3 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104v1)). Jack's current networks (thousands to about 100k parameters) are **10 to 100 times smaller than any published Dreamer score**.

**What to expect.** In Jack's current rover world, a correctly built Dreamer arm should show first meals within 10⁴–10⁵ decisions and regular foraging by about 10⁵–10⁶ (inference). The anchors are Animal-AI 3 foraging (about 90k steps to 18 of 22 from pixels) ([Animal-AI 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11414)) and EVAAA, a 3-D needs-based survival platform. There, DreamerV3's survival curve sat at 1,000–8,000 steps for about 180 episodes before jumping above 40,000. The research notes loosely put that jump at 0.3–0.6M steps (inference); the platform's authors give no step axis ([EVAAA](https://www.proceedings.com/content/085/085713-2094open.pdf)). At about 10⁶ steps on a pixel survival world, expect **about 14.5% Crafter score at 200M parameters** and less when small ([DreamerV3 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104v1)). At about 10⁷, expect **35–40%** (37M–200M) ([EMERALD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04075)). At about 10⁸ in a 3-D sparse world, expect most early milestones and occasional deep ones (Minecraft return 9.1 of 12) ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)). On 3-D embodied survival with a curriculum, expect only about **0.2 of human at 5×10⁷**, no better than model-free agents ([Avalon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13417)).

### Candidate B: a replay-based model-free actor-critic

**How it works.** The brain keeps a large memory of everything it has lived through. At every step it samples a batch from that memory and nudges a critic ("how good is this action here?") and an actor ("what should I do?"). It never predicts the world. For Jack's single, slow, continuous life, the family's natural member is **SAC** (an actor-critic that also rewards keeping some randomness in its actions). The modern stabilisers are layer normalisation, running input normalisation and residual blocks, as in SimBa or BRO. Its large-scale cousin is recurrent PPO. A GRU or LSTM can sit at the core. Off-policy recurrent agents beat specialised memory methods on 18 of 21 partially observed tasks ([Ni et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05038)).

**Track record.** Small recurrent model-free networks of **1.2–1.6M parameters** learned 30 DMLab tasks at once ([IMPALA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561)). The same size learned tool use in hide-and-seek, but only after **31.7 billion frames**. Batches of 8,000 and 16,000 chunks "never converged" ([Baker et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.07528)). That is a direct published warning against small-batch on-policy learning from one slow world. The replay-based branch is what matters for Jack. On the wheeled-robot lidar Goal task, closest in body and senses to Jack's rover (16 lidar bins, continuous actions), SAC, TD3 and DDPG rise above a return of 20 between **30k and 100k steps**. PPO needs **0.8–1.5M** for similar levels ([OmniSafe](https://github.com/PKU-Alignment/omnisafe/blob/main/benchmarks/off-policy/README.md); [Safety Gym](https://cdn.openai.com/safexp-short.pdf)). On ray-sensed food collection, a DQN of about 6,900 weights reaches the solved level after about 138k steps ([Danforth report](https://github.com/iandanforth/deeprl-nav/blob/master/Report.md); [Udacity](https://github.com/udacity/deep-reinforcement-learning/blob/master/dqn/solution/model.py)) — a community result with few seeds. A wheeled-style quadruped learned to walk in the real world in **20 minutes** at 20 Hz, about 24,000 decisions ([A Walk in the Park](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07860)).

**Strengths.** It is simple, cheap and fast. It is designed for a single data stream. Its sample efficiency at 20k–500k steps is consistently ahead of PPO ([Benchmarking MBRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02057)). And it has the most direct evidence on the exact current task.

**Weaknesses.** In head-to-head tests it needed roughly **10× to 200× more experience** than world-model agents for comparable scores: 10× on DMLab ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)) and 200× for PlaNet against A3C ([PlaNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04551)). Its model-free versions got **worse** when given an extra sense such as language ([Dynalang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01399)). PPO, its on-policy cousin, has no memory of old data. On a single stationary Ant task, standard PPO "increased for about 3 million steps but then collapsed" ([Dohare et al., Nature 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7)). Replay alone does not solve hard exploration: SAC with curiosity still scored −1.0 on the sparse Pyramids task ([Unity ML-Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02627)).

**What it needs.** It needs a replay buffer that holds the whole run (10⁶ by default, and never below about 50,000), batches of 256, about one gradient step per decision, a stochastic actor, and observation normalisation. Layer normalisation in the critic is advised too ([SAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05905); [SimBa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09754)). The stochastic actor matters because deterministic actors can "deadlock" on sparse tasks ([Matheron et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.11679)).

**What to expect.** On the current rover, the inference from neighbouring tasks is first measurable improvement within about **10,000–50,000 decisions** and reliable collection around **100,000–200,000** ([RL Zoo](https://github.com/DLR-RM/rl-baselines3-zoo/blob/master/benchmark.md); [OmniSafe](https://github.com/PKU-Alignment/omnisafe/blob/main/benchmarks/off-policy/README.md)). PPO would need about 1–2M ([Food Collector config](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/ml-agents/blob/release_12/config/ppo/FoodCollector.yaml)). On pixel survival worlds, the family scores **4.6% (untuned PPO) to 15–22% (tuned)** at 10⁶ ([Crafter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06780); [Moon et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03486)), about **0.17–0.20 of human** on 3-D survival at 5×10⁷ with a curriculum, and **0 without one** ([Avalon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13417)). Emergent tool use appears only at 10¹⁰ frames ([Baker et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.07528)).

### Candidate C: a decoder-free latent world model (TD-MPC2)

**How it works.** Like Dreamer, it learns an internal model, but it never tries to redraw its senses. It learns only a compact state that predicts reward and value. Before each action it tries 512 short action sequences, 3 steps ahead, inside that model ([TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)).

**Track record and strengths.** It covered **104 continuous-control tasks with one set of settings**, using about **5M parameters** and only one gradient update per step ([TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)). On Humanoid and Dog, "SAC and DreamerV3 are prone to numerical instabilities in Dog tasks, and are significantly less data-efficient than TD-MPC2 in Humanoid tasks" ([TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)). A Dreamer-compatible version, R2-Dreamer, swaps the image decoder for a redundancy-reduction loss. It trains **1.59× faster** than DreamerV3 and gains most on tasks with tiny relevant objects ([R2-Dreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18202)).

**Weaknesses.** "Extending TD-MPC2 to discrete action spaces remains an open problem". The published agent has no recurrent memory, and it "relies on rewards for task learning" ([TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)). Its famous scaling curve, from 1M (score 16.0) to 317M (70.6), was trained **offline** on 545M stored transitions rather than learned by living ([TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)). Its online many-task successor needed demonstrations and pretrained image and text encoders, both of which Jack's rules forbid ([Newt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19584)).

**What to expect.** Expect the best published sample efficiency on high-joint-count continuous bodies within about **10⁶–10⁷ steps**. Even so, expect whole-body work with hands to fail on flat training: on the 61-action HumanoidBench, every baseline, TD-MPC2 included, stayed "below the success threshold on most tasks" after about 48 hours ([HumanoidBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10506)). Its place is as the motor layer for the human-like body, or its decoder-free idea folded into Candidate A. It is not a candidate for the whole creature today.

### Recommendation and its strongest counter-argument

**Build Jack on Candidate A, test it against Candidate B as the control, and reserve Candidate C (or the decoder-free swap inside A) for the human-like body.** Three features of the evidence make A the default. It is the only design with one configuration across pixels, vectors and needs. It is the only one that has learned on physical bodies inside the step range Jack can afford. And it is the only one whose fused state has been shown to profit from added senses and words. The project's rule of "one brain fed by every sense" is the Dreamer architecture as published.

The strongest counter-argument is that **"as simple as possible" and "free compute only" both point at B**. For the current rover task, B has more direct evidence (ray-sensed foraging and lidar goal tasks at 10⁵ steps). It costs perhaps two orders of magnitude less computation per step. And it has no published small-size gap: a 2×64 SAC already solves a sparse continuous task, whereas Dreamer's 1M preset has no published score at all. Avalon found Dreamer and model-free agents equal on 3-D survival at 5×10⁷ steps ([Avalon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13417)). On EVAAA's easiest level, "DQN converged faster than DreamerV3", although only Dreamer then generalised to harder levels with a curriculum ([EVAAA](https://www.proceedings.com/content/085/085713-2094open.pdf)). The honest reading: on the current rover, B may well forage first. A earns its place on what comes next — eyes, ears, words, a high-joint-count body — where the record favours a predictive shared state. That is why both must be in the first fair race.

## One brain, many senses: how each design would take in sight, sound, memory and words

Every large from-scratch agent that mixes senses uses the same simple pattern. Each sense gets **one small encoder**, the encoder outputs are **concatenated**, and the result feeds **one recurrent core**. IMPALA ([IMPALA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561)), MERLIN ([MERLIN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10760)), OpenAI Five ([OpenAI Five](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680)), AdA ([AdA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07608)) and DreamerV3 ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)) all work this way. Hearing has one clean result. In audio-visual navigation, binaural spectrograms went through their own CNN, were concatenated with vision, and fed a GRU. Adding sound lifted navigation success from 0.601 to 0.709 with depth, and from 0.480 to 0.681 for a blind agent ([SoundSpaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.11474)). Joint vision-and-touch reconstruction "improve[s] sample efficiency" and even helps vision-only policies ([M3L](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00924)).

The documented risk is **modality dominance**. Multi-sense networks "tend to rely on just one modality while under-fitting the other modalities" ([Wu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05306)). In RL, multimodal input "can even fail to match the performance of using a single modality" ([You & Liu 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17551)). HumanoidBench's learners "fail to ignore" irrelevant hand senses during walking ([HumanoidBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10506)). The published guards suit a small from-scratch brain. Make the brain predict each sense with normalised losses. Drop whole senses at random during training ([Sensor Dropout](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10422)). Add a cross-sense consistency term ([MuMMI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02339)). Then check what each sense contributes by switching it off. Only Candidate A has the per-sense prediction built in, which is the strongest reason it should carry the multi-sense plan.

| Faculty | A. Dreamer-style | B. Model-free + recurrent core | C. TD-MPC2-style | Evidence and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eye (own camera) | CNN encoder, reconstructed or (R2-Dreamer) predicted without a decoder | CNN encoder into the core | Shallow CNN encoder, no decoder | Reconstruction misses small objects ([EMERALD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04075)); decoder-free fixes help ([R2-Dreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18202)) |
| Learned ear | Small CNN over a spectrogram-like array, predicted by the model | Same encoder, no prediction | Not published | Non-speech sound only ([SoundSpaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.11474)); **no raw-audio speech grounding published** |
| Touch, smell, taste, pain, temperature, needs | Symlog plus small dense network per sense, each predicted | Normalised vector into the core | Normalised vector | Health, hunger and breath scalars in Minecraft ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)); smell, taste, pain **untested at scale** |
| Short memory | GRU inside the model; carry state across replayed chunks | GRU/LSTM with state stored in replay, burn-in, previous action and reward as inputs | None in published agent | GRU is "the best general-purpose memory model" ([POPGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01859)) |
| Long memory (hundreds+ steps) | Swap GRU for an S4 sequence model (R2I) | Add attention over stored activations | — | R2I is superhuman on Memory Maze ([R2I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04253)); LSTMs falter near 250 steps ([Ni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03864)) |
| Words | One token per step as another sense; model predicts future words (Dynalang) | Text encoder into the core; known to degrade with hints | Not published | Reward-only word learning "exhibited no learning even after millions of training episodes" ([Hermann et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06551)) |
| Continual learning | Replay built in; add reservoir retention, LayerNorm, weight decay | Off-policy: replay built in; PPO needs L2 + unit resets | Replay built in | Replay "can be naturally extended to continual learning" ([Kessler et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15944)) |
| Shrinks gracefully? | 12M = 200M on control; about half return at 8M on Crafter | Yes in parameters, no in data | 1M weak on many tasks (16.0 vs 49.5 at 5M) | [DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104); [TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828) |

**Words will be the most expensive faculty.** Grounding words from nothing has repeatedly needed a predictive objective on the language channel. Reward alone failed even after millions of episodes, and word learning was "much faster once some words are already known" ([Hermann et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06551)). In a grid world with a synthetic language, single instruction levels took **15,900 to 1.6 million episodes** with RL ([BabyAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08272)). Dynalang ran 50M steps on its HomeGrid task, and models embedding one-hot tokens from scratch "struggle to learn the complex language … without pretraining on" a simpler stage ([Dynalang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01399)). For a parent's voice, the most relevant passive result is a model trained only on one child's head-camera footage: **61 hours** of video and 37,500 transcribed utterances. It reached 61.6% on a 4-way test of 22 concepts, but only 38.0% with a frozen random vision encoder ([Vong et al., Science 2024](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi1374)). The order follows: **words come after the eye works**, simple words first, and the project should expect to be first in the world at grounding words from raw speech.

**Memory across lives and continual learning** rest on add-ons that work with any design. A replay store that keeps old lives by reservoir sampling "almost eliminat[es] catastrophic forgetting", and stayed effective when shrunk from 450M to 5M frames ([CLEAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)). Plasticity is the other half. "A combination of layer normalization and weight decay is highly effective" ([Lyle et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18762)). For PPO-style learners, continual regularisers beat periodic resets ([Juliani & Ash 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19153)). Published onsets of plasticity loss are late (about 2–3M steps for PPO on Ant), so plasticity loss does not explain the 40,000-decision race ([Dohare et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11338828/)). No published agent keeps a memory across episodes except through its weights or replay. Carrying recurrent state across a death is non-standard, and it should be tested against clearing it.

## Why the seven-learner race could not separate anything

### Forty thousand decisions sits below every published foraging budget

The smallest published budgets at which a from-scratch agent reliably collects food are about **60k–170k decisions**. All come from replay-based learners on food-dense or distance-shaped tasks. PPO-type learners sit at 0.2–2M ([Animal-AI 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11414); [Safety Gym](https://cdn.openai.com/safexp-short.pdf)), and sparse or homeostatic versions at 2–50M and beyond ([JSAI 2023](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/pjsai/JSAI2023/0/JSAI2023_1Q4OS7b01/_pdf); [Avalon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13417)). The table places 40,000 decisions on that scale.

| Published reference | Experience to reliable collection | 40k as a share | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ray-sensed Banana collector, DQN | about 138k steps | about 30% | [Danforth](https://github.com/iandanforth/deeprl-nav/blob/master/Report.md) (community, few seeds) |
| Lidar Goal task (rover-like), SAC/TD3 | about 100k steps | about 40% | [OmniSafe](https://github.com/PKU-Alignment/omnisafe/blob/main/benchmarks/off-policy/README.md) |
| Same task, PPO | 0.8–1.5M | 3–5% | [Safety Gym](https://cdn.openai.com/safexp-short.pdf) |
| Crafter standard budget | 1M | 4% | [Crafter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06780) |
| Homeostatic two-resource Ant, PPO | onset about 1.7M, long survival about 5M (estimated) | 1–2% | [JSAI 2023](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/pjsai/JSAI2023/0/JSAI2023_1Q4OS7b01/_pdf) |
| Avalon 3-D "eat", with curriculum | 50M | 0.08% | [Avalon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13417) |

What the published curves show at **the same budget** is the decisive point. Crafter's per-episode logs, recomputed, show that at 25–50k steps a cow is eaten in **2.2% (PPO), 4.2% (Rainbow) and 8.6% (DreamerV2)** of episodes, against 0.1–0.4% for random ([Crafter scores](https://github.com/danijar/crafter/tree/main/scores)). On Animal-AI 3 at 40–50k steps, PPO and Dreamer both sit inside the random agent's score range ([Animal-AI 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11414)). Banana DQN at 40k steps scores about +1 to +3 against a final +13 ([Danforth score plot](https://github.com/iandanforth/deeprl-nav/blob/master/images/solve-avg-13.png)). **The published record therefore predicted exactly what happened: no learner eats at 40,000 decisions, whatever its design.** Crafter's baselines reach about Jack's pass mark of 5 meals per 3,000 steps only at about 1M steps (2.7, 6.7 and 4.7 cows rate-normalised; inference) ([Crafter scores](https://github.com/danijar/crafter/tree/main/scores)). The same holds for curiosity and active inference. Every curiosity success found used **10⁷ to 10⁹ steps** ([RND](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12894); [HIRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08296)). Curiosity-only agents eat a Crafter cow in under 1% of episodes ([Crafter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06780)). And no from-scratch active-inference forager exists in the literature ([Mazzaglia et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.10083)).

### Two seeds cannot rank seven arms

The unit of replication is the run, not the life: 15 lives inside one run share their weights. That gives N = 2 per arm. "In almost all cases 5 runs is insufficient to make strong claims" ([Patterson et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01315)). At N = 2, some standard tests have zero power by construction ([Colas et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06979)). Detecting a difference of one standard deviation with 87% power takes about **20 seeds** ([Colas et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06979)). Two groups of 5 seeds of the *same* configuration once differed "significantly" (p = 0.0016) ([Henderson et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06560)). Every arm also scored within the random floor (best 0.4 against random 0–0.2 and a pass mark of 5), so the data support only one statement: **none learned within the budget**.

### The settings matched published failure conditions

The race's learners also repeated several published failure conditions. The research notes disagree slightly on the order of harm. The learner-settings note puts the tiny replay first. The reward note puts reward scale and normalisation first. The exploration note shows white noise as an all-or-nothing failure on the closest small task. The table below ranks the settings by combining these. The ranking is inference: no paper ablates these settings together.

| Rank | Current setting | Why the record says it costs | Published effect size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Budget of 40k decisions, 2 seeds** | Below every foraging budget; underpowered by 10× in seeds | See tables above |
| 2 | **Replay of 256, batch 16, one step per 4–8 decisions** (each decision studied about 2–4 times before it is evicted, 51 s later) | Neural learners with tiny or no replay "fail to learn anything" ([Zhang & Sutton](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01275)); replay-free SAC/PPO score **0.04/0.07** of normal across 50 tasks even after tuning 30 configurations ([Streaming Deep RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14606)); AWR, the base of the current policy loss, was already unstable at 5,000 ([AWR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00177)) | Largest learner-side cost |
| 3 | **Gaussian white noise redrawn every decision** | On an integrating body, consecutive random commands cancel; the team's probe moved 1.3 m in 50 s. | 4 | **Discount 0.95 (4-second horizon)** | A meal 10 s away keeps 7.7% of its value (60.5% at γ = 0.99); about the same as the unavoidable metabolic cost over those 10 s (inference). | 5 | **Reward scale and per-64-decision advantage normalisation** | A meal at energy 0.95 pays 0.0025; the published homeostatic agents multiply by 100 ([trp_env](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ugo-nama-kun/trp_env/main/trp_env/envs/two_resource_env.py)). At 0.4 meals per life, at most 1 segment in 117 contains a meal, so over 99% of normalised advantage is rescaled noise (inference); "scaling up advantages when rewards are sparse can amplify noise" ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)); normalisation "matters a lot at the smallest batch sizes" ([Hilton et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00641)) | Low to high; the notes disagree |
| 6 | **"World-model" arms with no action input and no imagination** | Not Dreamer by the published definition; the team reviewer's view that five of seven arms were the same learner is consistent with that definition ([DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)) | Validity problem, not a tuning cost |
| 7 | **GRU called with zero state every step** | Equivalent to a feed-forward network; memory-free agents tie or win on navigation benchmarks ([POPGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01859)) | Lowest direct cost; tested nothing about memory |

**The reward form itself is not the main culprit.** "Drive before minus drive after" is the Keramati–Gutkin homeostatic reward ([Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)). It is also the form that Yoshida and colleagues found "yields the best results" among five ([Yoshida et al., code README](https://github.com/ugo-nama-kun/homeostatic_agents_pfrl)). In their curves it learned about three times faster than the plain deviation reward, and a death-only reward never learned ([JSAI 2023](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/pjsai/JSAI2023/0/JSAI2023_1Q4OS7b01/_pdf)). But it carries a trap the team should know about. Summed without discounting, every life from full to starved totals exactly the same, so **only the discount separates eating from not eating**. The policy-relevant signal is a running cost of (1−γ)×drive per step ([Ng, Harada & Russell 1999](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/icml99-shaping.pdf); [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)). Combined with a 4-second horizon and a near-zero payment for eating when nearly full, that leaves almost nothing to learn from. The tanh-squashed advantage-weighted policy loss also invites saturated actions, a documented route to runs that "never recover" ([Bjorck et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11222)).

### Known-answer checks come before any race

Every source on RL debugging opens the same way: "broken RL code almost always fails silently" ([Spinning Up](https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/spinningup/spinningup.html)). The cheapest gate is the five **probe environments**, tiny tasks with hand-computable answers that isolate the value loss, backpropagation, discounting, the policy update and batching. Each runs in "a second or two" ([Andy Jones](https://andyljones.com/posts/rl-debugging.html)). Next comes a ladder of small public tasks with tuned reference budgets:

- **CartPole-v1** reaches 500 in 50–100k steps.
- **Pendulum-v1** reaches about −157 in 20k steps with SAC; this is the continuous-control check.
- **MountainCarContinuous-v0** reaches 88–95 in 20–50k steps, but only with exploration-specific settings; it is the nearest small analogue of "nothing happens until you reach food" ([RL Zoo benchmark](https://github.com/DLR-RM/rl-baselines3-zoo/blob/master/benchmark.md)).
- **bsuite's** deep_sea, umbrella_length and memory_len probe directed exploration, delayed credit and memory; each experiment runs in under 30 CPU-minutes ([bsuite](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03568)).

A Dreamer arm has **no published small-task score** at Jack's sizes. Its gate is therefore relative: match model-free learners on the same small tasks, and match the official code's outputs component by component on the same seed ([Huang et al. 2022](https://iclr-blog-track.github.io/2022/03/25/ppo-implementation-details/)). The streaming-RL repository ships known-answer scripts for CartPole, MinAtar and Ant for any no-replay arm ([streaming-drl](https://github.com/mohmdelsayed/streaming-drl)).

### A first proof that the task can be learned at all

Learnability should be shown separately from any learner ([Patterson et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01315)). The record supports a three-part check.

First, a **hand-written agent that uses only Jack's own senses**. The scripted forager eating 24–27 per life uses privileged positions, so it shows the world can be foraged, not that the rays are enough ([Andy Jones](https://andyljones.com/posts/rl-debugging.html)).

Second, **behaviour cloning of the scripted forager into the learners' own network and inputs**, evaluated in closed loop and corrected with DAgger rounds (the clone drives while the expert labels). Open-loop cloning errors compound as the square of episode length; DAgger restores a linear bound ([Ross et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686)). A clone that eats shows that the senses, the action interface and the network size are sufficient. A clone that fails says that 16 undifferentiated distance rays may not tell food from walls. No published source answers that question.

Third, a **"cheat" learner given a vector to the nearest food**. If it cannot learn fast, the learner is at fault, not the task ([Andy Jones](https://andyljones.com/posts/rl-debugging.html)). The privileged-teacher decomposition went from 20 to 100 on its driving benchmark ([Learning by Cheating](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12294)).

By Schulman's rule, a random policy eating 0–0.2 per life is close to "never does the right thing". That predicts failure without denser food, closer food or a curriculum ([notes on Schulman's talk](https://github.com/williamFalcon/DeepRLHacks)).

### Commit to a prediction before each run

Pre-registration has been piloted at NeurIPS ([NeurIPS 2020 workshop](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2020/workshop/16158)), and RL methodology lists HARKing (forming a hypothesis after the results are known) as a common error ([Patterson et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01315)). Each race entry should record:

- the closest published result (task, algorithm, steps, score) and the outcome it predicts at the planned budget;
- one primary metric (food per life, counted while learning, as in online-learning evaluations) ([EARL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09605));
- the calibration arms (random, scripted, cheat, clone);
- the seed count and the pilot that justified it;
- what result would mean "learner broken" (fails its gate) versus "task too hard" (passes the gate, the clone eats, and RL still does not).

Had this rule existed, the last race would have recorded "predicted: no arm learns at 40k". That prediction would have cancelled the race.

## What the record does not answer, and the only experiments worth the compute

The literature leaves specific holes. No published system combines Jack's features: a wheeled body, 16 undifferentiated rays, three needs, 0.2 s decisions and about 3,000-decision lives. No Dreamer or TD-MPC2 score exists below 8M parameters on any task like this. No throughput figures exist for the project's hardware. Nobody has compared linear and quadratic drives, or tested an intake term, on learning speed. Nobody has studied memory carried across deaths or grounded words in raw speech. These are the experiments worth running, cheapest first. Costs use the project's figures: about 1.2M decisions per 12-hour CPU kernel, up to 5 kernels at once, about 30 GPU-hours per week.

The record also says what **not** to spend compute on. Do not run more 40,000-decision races. Do not add curiosity bonuses while food is reachable by persistent noise: on Craftax "none of the tested exploration methods improved performance" ([Craftax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16801)). Do not build an active-inference or predictive-coding stack, for which no large result exists. Do not make networks bigger without the stabilisers. In RL, naively scaled networks "can rarely maintain performance, let alone improve it" ([BBF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19452)), and an unstabilised 265M model lost to a 23M one ([AdA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07608)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. The race was neither a test of brains nor evidence that Jack's world is unlearnable. It was a run below the published floor, with settings that every relevant paper flags. The result was decided before it started, and that result is itself the strongest argument for writing down a literature prior before each run. The brain question has a clear answer. A predictive world model is the only published design built for "one brain, every sense, learning by living". Its unknowns are concentrated in two places, small size and free hardware, and two cheap measurements (E3 and the E4 pilot) can close them.

The deeper implication is about order. Published foragers succeed through the training setup more than through architecture: food that pays when eaten, a horizon measured in tens of seconds, exploration that actually travels, food placed close at first, and replay large enough to remember a meal. Avalon's 0.001 against 0.716 for curriculum versus none dwarfs any architecture gap in the record. Jack's team should fix the setup first, prove each implementation and the task's learnability, and then let A and B race at a million decisions. After that the record runs out. Raw-speech words, memory across lives and many-sense fusion beyond four channels are open research, and the project should expect to produce that evidence rather than find it.
