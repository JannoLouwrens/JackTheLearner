# Prove each part before Jack races again

The record already holds most of what Jack needs, and it changes what the team should do first. Fix his world before judging any brain. Run every brain from published code until it hits a published number. Then spend the team's own compute on a short list of questions nobody has answered. Five findings decide the order. First, **his world's economy is the outlier of every survival world reviewed**. A floor meal restores 8% of the energy bar where published teachable worlds give 44–100%. The team's own constants show that the easy food only delays death, so a sustained life needs the hardest skill, the ladder apple ([crafter objects.py](https://github.com/danijar/crafter/blob/HEAD/crafter/objects.py); [Avalon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13417)). Second, **brain A, the Dreamer-style world model, is limited by its learner, not by the simulator**. At the published amount of training per decision it cannot run on a free CPU (inference). Its one affordable published check costs an estimated 25–60 hours of the free T4, about one to two weeks of quota ([official score file](https://github.com/danijar/dreamerv3/blob/e01491fad6434b2245a3b8ca201dd7faedcc458c/scores/dmc_proprio-dreamerv3.json.gz)). That makes A, not B, the pacing item of the whole plan. Third, **the published "known answers" for brain B and PPO are single runs**. The real targets are seed distributions in a public run store, and several "solved" settings fail on 1 to 5 seeds in 10 ([Open RL Benchmark](https://wandb.ai/openrlbenchmark/sb3)). Fourth, **a fair A-against-B test fits in about 100 free CPU sessions, but it can only see large differences**: roughly a 50-point gap in the share of runs that learn to forage, or a three- to fourfold difference in speed ([Patterson et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01315); own power calculations in the notes). Fifth, **the released AdaStop statistics package doubled its stated false-alarm rate** in a check made for these notes. Every statistical tool must pass a same-against-same test before it is trusted ([adastop repository](https://github.com/TimotheeMathieu/adastop)). Beyond stage 2 the record is rich for memory, sleep, the body, the ear and words. It goes thin at one point: what a creature that dies and is reborn in one persistent world keeps between lives, and how it learns to weigh advice that is sometimes false. That is the team's real last 10%.

## How to read this report

This report is the build manual for the four stages of the plan. Every section follows the plan's five steps for each part: what the world already tested (how it works, what to expect in numbers), what to take as published and combine, which published number our build must reproduce, the last 10% as designed experiments, and how each part goes into him. Sources are linked inline. Unmarked facts were read in a primary paper, table, code file or configuration. Other evidence carries a label. **(inference)** marks arithmetic or reasoning from published numbers that nobody measured. **(figure reading)** marks values read off a plot by eye, so they are approximate. **(secondary)** marks a source that reports someone else's result. **[unverified]** marks claims the researchers could not confirm in a primary source. **(measured here, under load)** marks timings taken on the project's 4-core ARM server while it was busy with other work, so they are upper bounds on cost, not published figures. **(one preprint)** marks a single unreviewed result.

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Decision | One action choice by Jack: 0.2 s of his life (40 physics sub-steps) |
| Run, seed | One independent learner living its whole budget (many lives) from its own random start; the unit of evidence |
| Brain A | A Dreamer-style world model. It learns to predict its own next senses and reward, then trains its actor ("what to do") and critic ("how good is this") on futures imagined inside that model |
| Brain B | A replay learner (the SAC family, Soft Actor-Critic). It keeps everything it lived in a replay buffer and re-studies it to learn action values directly, with no model of the world |
| PPO | Proximal Policy Optimisation. A standard learner that uses each piece of experience a few times and then discards it; the reference, not a candidate |
| Train ratio / update ratio | How much training the learner does per decision lived. For Dreamer, a train ratio of 1024 means one gradient step per decision at its standard batch |
| Gradient step / update | One adjustment of the network's weights from one batch of data |
| Kernel-session | One free 12-hour Kaggle CPU session (4 x86 cores) |
| Calibration arm | A run that must fail (random policy, statue) or must succeed (scripted forager) for the experiment to count |
| Censored | A run that never reached the threshold within its budget. It is kept and counted as "not yet", never dropped |

## Five findings change what the team does first

**His world is built in the reverse order of every world that taught survival.** Published teachable worlds make the first rung sustain life. Crafter has a cow worth 67% of the food bar and unlimited water a median 7 cells from the start. Avalon puts a whole-bar fruit 0.5 m in front of the agent at its easiest setting. The two-resource ant gets by with 10% meals only because ten items sit in a 144 m² arena, a sensor reports each food type across the whole arena, eaten food is replaced instantly, and a life without food lasts 6,667 steps ([crafter objects.py](https://github.com/danijar/crafter/blob/HEAD/crafter/objects.py); [avalon eat.py](https://github.com/Avalon-Benchmark/avalon/blob/HEAD/avalon/datagen/world_creation/tasks/eat.py); [trp_env](https://github.com/ugo-nama-kun/trp_env/blob/HEAD/trp_env/envs/two_resource_env.py)). In Jack's world the researchers read the project's own `drives.py`. Two floor foods worth 0.08, each on a 66.9 s timer, supply at most 0.0024 of the bar per second. The file's own comments say that funds "a duty cycle of D* = 0.217" and is "2.09x short of constant activity". The ladder apple supplies about 62% of the maximum possible intake. Those constants were calibrated on the humanoid's power use, so the rover figures need confirming. If they hold, a learner must master the hardest skill before it can live long enough to learn anything else (inference). That one fact can make any brain comparison meaningless. So the world gets fixed and tested before stage 2, with published rules that have measured effects. Make the first meal big and near. Re-place eaten food at once. Draw a new layout every life. Give each skill a success-gated difficulty dial, which turned 0.001 into 0.716 on Avalon's eating task ([Avalon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13417)).

**Brain A's cost is set by its learner, and a CPU cannot afford the published training rate.** The official Dreamer agent at its standard batch (16 sequences of 64 steps) does one gradient step per decision at train ratio 1024. No throughput has been published for a T4, a CPU or Apple silicon. The notes' arithmetic predicts 20–50 gradient steps per second for the 0.64M-parameter `size1m` preset on a T4, 2–5 for the 9.7M `size12m`, and only 0.5–2 for `size1m` on a 4-core CPU, each good to about a factor of two (inference). At ratio 1024 on a CPU that means 0.5–2 decisions per second, against a world that runs 33–38 per second per core. Cutting the ratio is not free: one user report has Walker Walk falling from about 900 to 200–300 when the ratio dropped from 512 to 32 ([dreamerv3 issue 140](https://github.com/danijar/dreamerv3/issues/140)). In practice brain A lives on the T4, and later on the Mac mini. The first review assumed a simulator-bound 10 hours per million decisions. The plan has to be recosted around roughly 8–14 T4-hours per million-decision run of `size1m` at full ratio (inference), within a 30-hour weekly quota that is used up until 21 November.

**The published targets for B and PPO are distributions, not single runs.** The RL Zoo table that the first review quoted ("Pendulum about −157 in 20k steps") is one training run per entry, often the luckiest. The Open RL Benchmark store holds 10–22 seeds of the same settings, and those are the honest targets. SAC on Pendulum-v1 at 20,000 steps averages −298.4 ± 10.3 over all 100 training episodes (13 seeds). SAC solves the sparse MountainCarContinuous on 9 of 10 seeds. DQN on CartPole reaches 392 ± 150 where the table says 500 ([Open RL Benchmark, sb3 project](https://wandb.ai/openrlbenchmark/sb3); [RL Zoo benchmark.md](https://github.com/DLR-RM/rl-baselines3-zoo/blob/d802099/benchmark.md)). A home-made learner judged against the single-run table can look broken when it is correct, or correct when it is lucky.

**A correct comparison is affordable but blunt.** With about 20 runs per arm, exact tests and two pre-declared looks, 100 kernel-sessions detect a fourfold speed-up with 93–100% probability. A twofold speed-up is found only 34–63% of the time, and a 30-point gap in success rate usually comes out "not distinguished". The notes simulated this themselves on published methods (own calculation; [Lakens, sequential designs](https://lakens.github.io/statistical_inferences/10-sequential.html); [Mathieu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10882)). The team should decide in writing, before stage 2, that "not distinguished" means "choose on cost and simplicity", not "equal".

**The tools themselves must be tested.** AdaStop is the one stopping-early test written for comparing RL agents. In a same-against-same check (1,000 simulated comparisons of two identical agents), its PyPI release 0.1.3 declared a difference 9.7–11.8% of the time at a stated 5%. The repository version with a one-line fix from 17 October 2026 held 4.2–5.3%. No fixed release exists on PyPI ([adastop repository](https://github.com/TimotheeMathieu/adastop); own simulation in the notes). The same check caught a transposed table while the notes were being written. Every part of the pipeline gets the same treatment, from launching runs to the final p-value.

## Stage 1, the instrument: his world, the harness and two brains from published code

### His world: take the published rules, then prove the world teaches

**What the world already tested.** Every world in which from-scratch agents learned to survive follows one pattern: a big near first meal, a life capped at 30 times or more the time a passive agent takes to die (Crafter 10,000 against 338 steps; Neural MMO 1,024 against about 30), resources that can be sensed from the start position, reward on intake, and a new layout every life ([crafter env.py](https://github.com/danijar/crafter/blob/HEAD/crafter/env.py); [Neural MMO config](https://github.com/NeuralMMO/environment/blob/HEAD/nmmo/core/config.py)). Even so, the first resource takes 10⁵–10⁶ steps in grid worlds and 10⁷–10⁸ in 3-D ([Crafter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06780); [Avalon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13417)). Two staging methods have the largest measured effects. The first is the success-gated dial: each life's difficulty is drawn uniformly below a ceiling, the ceiling rises a little after a success and falls after a failure (Avalon 0.716 against 0.001 at 50M steps; a game unlearnable at full difficulty reached 57% wins in [Justesen et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10729)). The second is help that wears off, which beat reward shaping on every task where both were tried; a world that changes by itself raised success from 0% to 70% with no resets ([Ecological RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12478)). Agents memorise fixed layouts up into the thousands. The train-test gap closes only at about 10,000 distinct levels, and never repeating a level works best ([Procgen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01588)). Three degenerate optima are on record and threaten Jack's reward directly. Agents sat still for about 1.3 billion steps under a "keep needs above half" reward ([ForageWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06981)). Agents learned to end their lives when food could not be found, because EVAAA's reward is negative while needs decay ([EVAAA](https://www.proceedings.com/content/085/085713-2094open.pdf)). And hand-added sub-rewards pulled agents off task. Jack's reward is the reduction of a drive, which is negative on every step that a need worsens. The Dreamer notes infer that once living has negative expected value, the same self-termination should be expected (inference). The world's test card must check for it.

**Take as published and combine.** Eating by a distance test of 0.5–1.0 m, with eaten food re-placed at once at a new random position ([trp_env](https://github.com/ugo-nama-kun/trp_env/blob/HEAD/trp_env/envs/two_resource_env.py)). Rays that report what they hit; Jack's rays already return a per-object texture code, so the remaining gap is the hanging apple, which no horizontal ray can register. A smell-like signal that grows as 1/distance and a few thermal readings ([EVAAA](https://www.proceedings.com/content/085/085713-2094open.pdf)). Crafter's need arithmetic: several bars, each emptied in about 190–280 steps awake, health lost only while a bar is empty, drains at half speed asleep. Random starting needs each life ([crafter objects.py](https://github.com/danijar/crafter/blob/HEAD/crafter/objects.py)). Physics devices from hide-and-seek and AdA: grab as a weld constraint within 0.25 m, lock as frozen joints, transformations as "when A is near or touching B, remove both and spawn C" ([hide-and-seek manipulation.py](https://github.com/openai/multi-agent-emergence-environments/blob/HEAD/mae_envs/wrappers/manipulation.py); [AdA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07608)). Per-object temperature with cooked and burnt thresholds ([iGibson 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03272)). Scarcity becomes the dial, not the starting point. Meal size, item count, distance and respawn delay become functions of a ceiling d, with today's world near d = 1. A share of lives stays unassisted from the first day, and progress is judged only on fixed unassisted layouts at d = 1 (inference assembling the published parts).

**Reproduce first.** The notes list nine published results the rover's simulator should match in simple form. Three come first. The two-resource ant from the authors' `trp_env` with their PPO settings should survive about 49,700 of 60,000 steps after 41.7M steps. That number comes from the first review's notes and was not re-read in this round, so treat it as **[unverified]**. Next, drop the rover into the same arena (6 + 4 items in 12 × 12 m, 1.0 m eat radius, 0.1 per item, instant re-placement). If it does not reach the survival cap sooner than the ant, the fault lies in his senses, actions or learner, not in the world rules (inference). Finally, the curriculum contrast: uniform food distance should stay near zero while the gated ceiling clearly rises above it, matching Avalon's 0.001 against 0.716. A fourth check takes minutes on a CPU: environment shaping beating reward shaping in the authors' own 8 × 8 grid (0% against 70%) ([Ecological RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12478)).

**How a world is tested.** Designers run the same short ladder before trusting agents in a world. A do-nothing agent: how long until death. A random agent: does it ever get reward. A scripted agent using only the agent's own senses: the near-optimal reference. Then standard learners at a fixed budget, with watched replays to catch exploits ([Crafter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06780); [Animal-AI 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11414)). A world passes when random play sometimes earns reward but not enough, the scripted agent survives, and at least one standard learner separates from random within the budget. That ladder replaces the five world-rule races that shipped nothing.

### Brain A: take the official Dreamer whole, and reproduce its 2023 curves on the T4

**How it works and what to expect.** The 2024–25 "v2" agent of the Nature paper is fully defined by about 30 lines of configuration and about 850 lines of agent code in the official repository at commit `e01491f`. It uses an 8-block recurrent state with 32 categorical latents and a symlog vector encoder and decoder. Its reward and critic heads use "two-hot" outputs over 255 bins. A continuation head predicts whether life goes on. Its losses are weighted 1·reconstruction + 1·reward + 1·continue + 1·dynamics + 0.1·representation, with 1 free nat. Imagination runs 15 steps from all 1,024 replayed states. The actor is a REINFORCE actor (a policy-gradient estimator) with entropy 3e-4 and `max(1, S)` return scaling. Optimisation uses LaProp with adaptive gradient clipping at 4e-5, on batches of 16 × 64 and a 5-million-step replay ([configs.yaml at e01491f](https://github.com/danijar/dreamerv3/blob/e01491fad6434b2245a3b8ca201dd7faedcc458c/dreamerv3/configs.yaml); [DreamerV3 v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104v2)). For Jack's roughly 30-number vector input, `size1m` has about 0.64M trainable parameters and `size12m` about 9.7M (inference from the code). The paper's ablations name what is load-bearing. Without KL balancing and free bits there was zero learning on proprioceptive Reacher Hard. Without the `max(1, ·)` floor, sparse swing-up fell from 850 to 200. Without reconstruction the score fell from 100 to 27 (figure reading). The only raw small-scale data are 90 proprioceptive DeepMind Control runs (18 tasks × 5 seeds), and the researchers found the file **bit-identical between the 2023 and 2024 releases**. The published proprioceptive numbers therefore come from the 2023 v1 agent at size S (train ratio 512, action repeat 2, 4 environments), not from the 12M v2 agent and not from `size1m`. They show an 18-task mean of 442, 626 and 757 at 50K, 100K and 250K decisions. Sparse ball-in-cup is solved by all seeds within 75K decisions. Sparse cartpole swing-up is solved by 3 of 5 seeds within 250K ([score file](https://github.com/danijar/dreamerv3/blob/e01491fad6434b2245a3b8ca201dd7faedcc458c/scores/dmc_proprio-dreamerv3.json.gz)). Dreamer fails at directed exploration (BSuite deep_sea 0.00, exploration category 0.01), at long memory (BSuite memory 0.62) and when shrunk (12M needs 38M Crafter steps for what 400M reaches at 7M) ([DreamerV3 v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104v2)).

Death matters for Jack. Dreamer handles it with one Bernoulli "continue" head trained on the true terminal flag; nothing else is special-cased. **Only the v2 REINFORCE actor can learn from termination.** The v1 actor for continuous actions backpropagates through the dynamics, is the default in older ports, and cannot learn from death at all. The environment must also mark true death and administrative cut-offs differently. The one needs-based Dreamer precedent, EVAAA, used sheeprl's DreamerV3-S with discount 0.997 and drive-reduction reward. It survived best of the agents tested, agents without needs inputs did not learn, and some agents learned to kill themselves when food could not be found ([EVAAA](https://www.proceedings.com/content/085/085713-2094open.pdf)).

**Take as published.** Use two codebases. The official JAX repository at `e01491f` is the reference oracle: the definition of the agent plus its per-seed score files. **sheeprl 0.8.5 `dreamer_v3_5`** with `dreamer_v3_5_1M` or `_12M` is the practical PyTorch runner. It is the only maintained port with the v2 agent, it runs on CPU by default, and it saves weights, optimiser state and replay for resuming across 12-hour sessions ([sheeprl dreamer_v3_5_1M.yaml](https://github.com/Eclectic-Sheep/sheeprl/blob/main/sheeprl/configs/algo/dreamer_v3_5_1M.yaml)). The port is six weeks old and has no published scores, so it earns trust only through the checks below. r2dreamer cannot resume, and dreamerv3-torch is deprecated. For Jack: every published value unchanged; one environment; action repeat 1 (a decision is already 0.2 s); the train ratio as high as the hardware sustains up to 1024, and logged; `terminated` only on true death; a meal reward of order 1; needs, touch and rays as one vector; the REINFORCE actor; a 5,000–10,000-decision prefill; DayDreamer's two-process arrangement, where one process acts, one learns, and they share a replay directory ([DayDreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14176)). The main independent critique found that Dreamer's tricks do not transfer to PPO as general improvements. So the recipe is taken whole, never piecemeal.

**Reproduce first.** The protocol is the published one: v1 agent, size S, ratio 512, repeat 2, 5 seeds. A run passes if our mean is within about 2 standard deviations of the published mean at each point. Some figures in the battery notes differ from these (cartpole swing-up 528 ± 36 at 100K, attributed to a "12M model"), against the Dreamer notes' 478 ± 20 computed directly from the per-seed file. The Dreamer notes' figures below are the better-documented ones; recompute from the file before use.

### Brain B and the PPO reference: maintained libraries, tuned entries, seed distributions

**How it works and what to expect.** Plain SAC with two hidden layers of 256 units and 2 gradient updates per decision reaches 853.0 ± 9.2 on DeepMind Control cartpole swing-up after 50,000 decisions, and 968 and 956 on the two reacher tasks after 150,000 (10 seeds, public per-seed file) ([SimBa results/sac.csv](https://github.com/SonyResearch/simba/blob/7d0358b1f1fa3404fc5ea0ffedf9dc1424b49a45/results/sac.csv)). On the sparse swing-up it is unreliable early: 93.8 ± 166.1 at 50K decisions and 776.5 ± 30.2 at 150K. Newer critic-heavy methods (CrossQ, BRO, SimBa, MR.Q) gain mostly on high-dimensional tasks and were measured on GPUs. On a CPU the best-supported choice is the plain configuration with a layer-normalised critic. **Gradient updates are not free on the project's CPUs** (measured here, under load). One SAC update at batch 256 costs about 50 ms of an ARM core in PyTorch Stable-Baselines3 and about 20 ms in SBX (the JAX port by the same maintainer) with 2×256 networks, or about 1.9 ms in SBX with 2×64. PPO is the slow, seed-fragile reference. Default SB3 PPO has a median of 127 across 28 DeepMind Control tasks at 500K decisions, where replay and model-based learners score 613–927. On sparse swing-up, three sources give 7, 76 and 391 ([MR.Q](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16142)). About four parallel environments is its published optimum ([Colored Noise in PPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11091)). Exploration that holds a direction is what solves the sparse classic task. The Zoo recipe for MountainCarContinuous is gSDE (state-dependent exploration noise held over several steps) with a *fixed* entropy coefficient of 0.1 and discount 0.9999 ([RL Zoo sac.yml](https://github.com/DLR-RM/rl-baselines3-zoo/blob/d802099/hyperparams/sac.yml#L8-L21)). Memory in a replay learner has one well-documented design: separate 128-unit GRU encoders for actor and critic, trained on 64-step windows. It reaches −168.6 ± 3.5 on velocity-only Pendulum at 51,000 steps, and it fails with a 5-step window (about −1,380) or a shared encoder (about −422) ([Ni et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05038)). For a two-dimensional drive, a Q-learner over 9 joint actions (3 levels per dimension) matches strong continuous agents on 1- and 2-dimensional control (figure reading; [DecQN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12566)).

**Take as published.** Stable-Baselines3 2.9.2 with sb3-contrib 2.9.2 for PPO and RecurrentPPO, and **SBX 0.28.0 for brain B**. SBX is the only maintained member of the family that exposes LayerNorm, dropout, policy delay, a separate critic learning rate, resets and SimBa blocks on SAC ([SBX README](https://github.com/araffin/sbx/blob/8d20429/README.md)). Brain B's settings: `layer_norm=True`, 2×256, learning rate 3e-4, batch 256, replay at least as long as the run (10⁶), tau 0.005, gamma 0.99 to start, 5,000–10,000 warm-up decisions, 1–2 updates per decision, normalised observations. Death is `terminated=True` and every administrative stop is `truncated=True`, so the libraries bootstrap correctly through time limits. Exploration is either the Zoo's sparse recipe or pink noise with a sequence as long as a life, never both. Both carry traps the notes found. The pink-noise package also changes the noise inside SAC's updates and consumes its sequence three times per step (measured here). SBX implements `use_sde` as a held noise draw, not as SB3's gSDE. So either device must pass the MountainCarContinuous reproduction in the library actually used ([pink-noise-rl sb3.py](https://github.com/martius-lab/pink-noise-rl/blob/7412366/pink/sb3.py#L79-L125)). PPO uses SB3 defaults with `VecNormalize`, four simulator processes and gSDE with a sample period of 4. A teacher showing food is published as data around the learner: keep one demonstration in replay permanently and draw a fixed share of each batch from it ([DDPGfD](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08817); [RLPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02948)).

### The simulator: physics is fast, the loop around it is slow

One loaded core of the project's server steps a stock MuJoCo humanoid 20,000–25,000 times a second and a stock wheeled car 85,000 times. Jack's world manages a few thousand sub-steps a second (measured here, under load). The published record names the two causes and what each fix is worth. The RK4 integrator costs 3.7–3.8× against implicitfast or Euler (measured here; the team measured 3.8×). Python game logic around a fast engine cost a published system 23× ([Madrona](https://madrona-engine.github.io/shacklett_siggraph23.pdf)), and the team measured per-sub-step Python at about 45% of its time. Batching many lives per process is worth 2–3× ([MJX documentation](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mjx.html)). The notes predict 500–2,000 rover decisions a second per 4-core kernel after these changes, against about 33–38 per core today (inference, to be measured). Changing the integrator, or moving per-sub-step forces to once per decision, changes outcomes, so the world's certificates must be re-run. The team should make the change once, before stage 2 data are collected, never in the middle of a comparison. During stage 2 both brains are limited by their learners (A on the T4; B at roughly 25–50 decisions per second per ARM core with 2×256 networks, measured here under load). The speed-up therefore matters most for PPO, which is simulator-bound with four processes, and for stage 3, where budgets run to 10⁷ decisions (inference).

### The harness must pass thirteen self-checks before any comparison is believed

Reference libraries guard correctness in three layers ([Stable-Baselines3 tests and changelog](https://github.com/DLR-RM/stable-baselines3/blob/0cdcd20/docs/misc/changelog.md); [CleanRL RLops with Open RL Benchmark](https://wandb.ai/openrlbenchmark/sb3)). The first is unit tests with a closed-form answer, such as the value of a never-ending reward stream, 1/(1 − γ). The second is tiny learning tests with a pass mark that run in seconds on every commit. The third is reference-curve regression against stored runs before any change that could affect performance. Almost every silent bug in their changelogs concerns episode boundaries, tensor shapes, target networks, squashed actions or normalisation ([Pardo et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00378)). The test-method notes turn this into thirteen self-checks for the harness itself. Same-against-same at the stated rate, through the real pipeline and stopping rule, over at least 1,000 repetitions. A deliberately handicapped copy detected with the planned power. Calibration arms landing where they must (random, statue, untrained twin and shuffled-input learner at the floor; scripted forager at the ceiling). Determinism and seed independence. Paired worlds, so that run index k gets identical first observations and food layouts in every arm. Resume across a kernel boundary equal to an uninterrupted run. Step accounting that counts warm-up and prefill. Life boundaries (the reward-forever world gives 1/(1 − γ); a true death gives no bootstrap). Evaluation that leaves training logs identical. Outcome code checked on synthetic logs, including edge cases. A frozen analysis script whose hash goes in the pre-registration. Statistical tools checked against a known case (4 of 15 against 0 of 15: Fisher p = 0.100, Boschloo p = 0.043). And no silent drops, so the runs analysed equal the runs launched.

### Proof that the task can be learned with his senses

The first review's three-part check still stands. First, a hand-written forager that uses only his own senses. Second, a behaviour clone of the scripted forager into the learner's own network, run in closed loop and corrected with DAgger rounds. Third, a "cheat" learner given the vector to food ([Ross et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686)). The control-arm notes add three measurements, about two kernel-hours in total, that come before any brain experiment (inference). How often a random policy and a direction-holding noise policy reach food in a 3,000-decision life; if it is under about 1 life in 20, no learner in the record is expected to start without help. How many decisions separate meals for a hand-written ray-follower: if the usual gap exceeds about 100 decisions, the horizon of γ = 0.99, then 0.995 is the better discount. And whether four simulator processes on one kernel really deliver four times the decisions.

## Stage 2, choosing the brain: one comparison, about 100 kernel-sessions, large effects only

**The design the record supports.** The notes assemble a three-stage design from standard parts ([Patterson et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01315); [Eimer et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01324); [Janner et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08253)). The combination is the notes' own proposal; no published RL experiment uses exactly this set. **Stage 0** (about 6 kernel-sessions): the harness checks above, each arm's reproduction gate, and throughput measurements that fix the budget T per run. **Stage 1** (cap 24): a pilot with equal tuning on reserved seeds. Each arm gets its published default plus three pre-listed alternatives, three seeds each, at half length. That yields the configurations to freeze, the spread of outcomes, the between-arm correlation on shared world seeds, and the threshold. Nothing from stage 1 enters the final test. **Stage 2** (cap 57): confirmation on fresh seeds, with 20 runs of A, 20 of B and 10 of PPO at frozen settings. The calibration arms are random, statue, untrained twin and scripted forager (3 runs each), plus a shuffled-input learner (5 runs). **Reserve** (13): infrastructure re-runs, plus one pre-declared extra. If A wins, B runs at A's update ratio (5 runs) to show that the model, not the extra training, is the reason ([Janner et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08253)).

**The outcome and the test.** The primary outcome is decisions until first reliable foraging: the end of the first W consecutive lives whose mean food per life reaches θ. The team's existing pass mark of 5, against a scripted forager's 24–27 and a random policy's 0–0.2, is the natural θ. Runs that never get there are censored at T. The primary test compares A with B, two-sided, using a permutation test on log-rank scores, with two looks: after 10 runs per arm and after 20, each at level 0.025. Log-rank tests whether one group crosses the threshold sooner, counting censored runs correctly. The exact recipe is in the notes and was checked against `scipy.stats.logrank`. The pre-declared sensitivity analysis is Boschloo's exact test on the number of runs that crossed by T ([scipy boschloo_exact](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.boschloo_exact.html)). A futility rule ends the comparison early: if neither arm has more than one run past threshold at the first look, stop and report "not learnable at this budget". The gates void the comparison if random, statue, untrained or shuffled arms succeed more than once, or if the scripted forager fails. Crashes resume from their checkpoint with the same seed. Divergence counts as an outcome, and no seed is ever replaced.

**What the statistics say about few runs.** On a single task, bootstrap intervals need 20–30 runs to reach their stated coverage ([Agarwal et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264); [Jordan et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16241)). Welch's t-test runs at about twice its nominal error rate when scores are skewed, and bootstrap tests should not be used below 50 runs ([Colas et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06979)). Exact tests on success counts never exceed their level, and permutation tests are exact for "same distribution" at any sample size, but they cannot reject at all below 4–5 runs per arm. Peeking after every batch of five without correction gave a 12% false-positive rate (own simulation). The best of eight identical settings, tried on three seeds each, looked 38 points better in success rate than it was (own simulation). Settings chosen on tuning seeds did four to five times worse on fresh seeds in published cases ([Eimer et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01324)). Removing a part at test time and watching the score fall does not show the part mattered. Removing 90% of pixels at random cost 13 points with retraining and 76 without ([Hooker et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10758)). The clean test retrains without the part.

**The battery of standard tests.** The capability notes assemble twelve small standard tasks on which all three families, or most of them, have published numbers. Two packages carry most of the weight because they are the only places where all three families were measured on the same task: bsuite (DeepMind's behaviour suite) and the DeepMind Control proprioceptive tasks. Family A's references come from 10M–200M-parameter models at GPU budgets. A's checks must therefore run at the shortest published points or be read relative to B.

## Stage 3, growing him: survival, memory and sleep, body, ear and words

### Survival rungs and needs

**Known, and what to expect.** The learnable order on record runs: drink or the adjacent resource first (by 5×10⁴–10⁵ steps in Crafter), mobile food later (12–26% of episodes at 10⁶), delayed farming never, even at 10⁹ ([Crafter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06780); [Craftax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16801)). In 3-D with a curriculum at 5×10⁷ steps, eating reaches 0.7 of human, moving to food 0.3–0.4, and tool and composed tasks 0.00–0.16 ([Avalon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13417)). Published multi-need agents combine needs in three ways: one drive whose reduction is the reward, a per-step cost on the current state, or needs that act only through a health bar. The state-based forms produced the documented stalls. The one head-to-head study favoured one learner per need over one summed reward, more so as needs went from four to eight ([ForageWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06981)). Sleep as a need has been learned only in grid worlds. From scratch in physics, fort-building took about 6×10⁹ steps and one shaped "put meat on the stove and wait" took under 2M. **No from-scratch agent has made fire, cooked for its own hunger, or used shelter for warmth.**

**Take as published.** The rungs in the order the record says is learnable: eat food at the mouth, drive to visible food, food and water, warmth from an existing fire, night and sleep, one-contact gathering, feed a fire, cook, then enter, close and finally build a shelter. Add needs in EVAAA's order: food and water, then temperature and damage, then predators and night. An adult's help is a world setting tied to the dial, absent for part of every life ([Ecological RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12478); [EVAAA](https://www.proceedings.com/content/085/085713-2094open.pdf)).

### Memory, sleep and learning for a lifetime

**Working memory.** The GRU remains the best-supported small core (POPGym aggregate 0.349 against 0.255 for an LSTM and 0.067 for a memoryless network). Linear-recurrent cores (FFM, S5, LRU) are the only ones that beat it on the same benchmarks while training faster ([POPGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01859); [FFM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04128)). Two limits matter more than the choice of cell. Recurrent agents trained by reward alone falter at memory lengths around 250 steps, and nothing assigns credit across 250 steps ([Ni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03864)). And memory trained on replay chunks shorter than the dependency does not form at all: zero success in R2I's ablation ([R2I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04253)). Stored recurrent state, a burn-in prefix (40 of 80 steps in R2D2), sequences that never cross a death, and previous action and reward as inputs are part of the recipe, not details ([R2D2](https://openreview.net/forum?id=r1lyTjAqYX)). When food is in view, expect no benefit from memory: on POPGym's navigation tasks the memoryless network won.

**The event store (his diary).** A separate store has two published payoffs. The first is fast early learning when it holds *values* keyed by a learned state embedding. Neural Episodic Control reached a 16.7% median human-normalised Atari score at 1M frames, where DQN was at −0.7%, and was overtaken by 40M ([NEC](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01988)). The second is recall over gaps a recurrent core cannot span. That worked only when what is written is trained by prediction or reconstruction ([MERLIN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10760); [HCAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14039)), and only at about 10⁹ steps. Retrieval is always by learned similarity, never by word matching. That explains why the team's diary scores zero on reworded questions: published practice keys memories on the world model's latent state and reads the nearest neighbours (inference applying NEC's and MERLIN's designs).

**Across lives.** Across-episode memory emerges only when three things hold together: the memory is not wiped, the objective counts reward across episodes, and the world differs between groups of episodes. RL² met all three and cut second-episode paths by 25%, from 52.4 to 39.1 steps, after training on 1,000 mazes ([RL-squared](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02779)). A 100-episode scene memory added only 2.1% ([Mezghani et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.05181)). In one world whose layout never changes, slow weights fed by retained replay carry "where the apple tree was" ([Mirowski et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03673)). The one death-specific failure on record is forgetting death: as deaths become rare they leave the replay and the agent drifts back into them. A small store of danger states kept forever fixes it ([Lipton et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01211)).

**Sleep.** Learning while not acting is the oldest quantified result in the field. In the Dyna maze, 50 planning updates per real step cut learning from about 25 episodes to 3 ([Sutton and Barto](http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf)). MuZero reached a 126.6% median Atari score from 20M frames with 99.5% of its updates on re-analysed old data ([MuZero Unplugged](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294)). Only one test asks whether concentrating those updates into rest periods beats spreading them (one preprint). There, 75,000 critic updates every 10,000 steps beat the same updates spread evenly, but only with dropout, and more than 75,000 per phase made things worse ([SPEQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08669)). "Sleep replay" without stored data reached 48% where keeping 0.75% of the data reached 80% ([Tadros et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34938-7)).

**Not forgetting, staying able to learn.** A 50-50 mix of new experience and reservoir-retained old experience restored 31.4, 8.0 and 18.1 against 18.0, 5.0 and 10.9 on three alternating tasks. The result was insensitive between 50-50 and 75-25 and held with 5M of 900M frames kept ([CLEAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)). Keep by reservoir or by coverage, never by surprise or reward ([Isele and Cosgun 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10269)). Replay is not a complete answer in every learner: SAC replaying everything on Continual World scored 0.12 against PackNet's 0.80 ([Continual World](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10919)). One preprint finds a 17M Dreamer keeps its world model while its actor forgets ([Nijjer 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19749), one preprint). To keep the network able to learn, the new cheap addition is a slow averaged copy to which the fast network is periodically reset. It raised BBF's Atari-100k score from 0.826 to 0.891 at no extra compute ([Hare and Tortoise](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02596)). Plain shrink-and-perturb made one learner worse (0.139 against 0.243).

**Reproduce first.** The Dyna maze (minutes on a CPU): about 25 episodes to near-optimal with no planning, 5 with n = 5, 3 with n = 50. The passive T-Maze, where an LSTM solves short lengths and falters by 250. POPGym, where the GRU must beat the memoryless network wherever memory is needed. bsuite memory_len with 64-step chunks failing past 64 and 256-step chunks succeeding ([R2I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04253)). Split MNIST with a 200-sample buffer, where replay gives 80.43 ± 1.89 against 19.60 with none ([Arani et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12604)). And RL² bandits with the hidden state kept within a trial: 78.7 against Gittins 78.3 at n = 100, k = 5.

### Body, movement and the reflex layer

**Known, and what it says about past failures.** Walking with no motion capture is routine only with thousands of parallel copies and PPO: 2×10⁷–4×10⁸ steps, and 13–18 minutes for 3×10⁷ steps on a free T4 in the one community measurement ([MJX tutorial](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/main/mjx/tutorial.ipynb); [PR 1465](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/pull/1465)). In a single world the best learners (TD-MPC2, BRO, SimBa) need about 1M steps for a first walk. Plain SAC, PPO, A3C and DreamerV3 sit at the random floor for 1–4 million steps on DeepMind Control's humanoid ([TD-MPC2 results](https://github.com/nicklashansen/tdmpc2/tree/main/results); [SimBa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09754)). **The humanoid walking test that has failed since August is what the record predicts at the team's budgets.** A CPU can do it with help: a 29-joint humanoid walked with no motion capture after 10–30 million samples with 3×64 networks, in 4–15 hours on 8 threads, given a mirror-symmetry loss and helping forces that are withdrawn. Without the help it only stood or fell ([Yu et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08093)).

The control-path contest is largely answered too. In the two direct tests, a bigger actor did not help or hurt, while a bigger critic helped ([SimBa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09754); [BRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16158)). Working humanoid controllers are small (4×32 up to 512-256-128 weights per layer), and the largest from-scratch model acting every step on a humanoid is about 5M parameters. The team's reading of its contest (the 0.5M controller leads the 57M trunk) is what the record predicts, though the exact 57M contest is not in print. **A 0.2 s brain needs a reflex layer.** Published slow brains decide 6–10 times a second over controllers running at 30–200 a second, and no humanoid balances on decisions slower than 15–20 a second ([Director](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04114); [Heess et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05182)). Hands on a walking humanoid are unsolved from scratch. Every HumanoidBench manipulation task misses its target, and tool use has emerged only with an abstract grab action ([HumanoidBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10506)).

**Take and reproduce.** A reflex walking controller with no motion capture: PD joint targets (desired angles turned into torques by a fast fixed loop) at 20–50 a second, the reward terms of the Playground G1 joystick or Yu's three terms with the mirror loss, random pushes, and a small MLP. The rover-trained brain sits on top, emitting a velocity command every 0.1–0.2 s ([MuJoCo Playground](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08844)). Reproduce, cheapest first. The Gymnasium Humanoid-v5 floor: random actions 110 ± 30 in 23.5 steps (measured here). The MJX tutorial humanoid on a T4 within 1.5× of 13–18 minutes. SAC on Gymnasium Humanoid at about 5,200–5,500 after 1M steps ([Tianshou benchmark](https://tianshou.org/en/v1.1.0/01_tutorials/06_benchmark.html)). Yu's CPU recipe walking unaided with a symmetry index of about 0.01, in one 12-hour kernel. Locomotion tests should report distance, survival time, cost of transport, symmetry and push recovery, because return alone has rewarded rolling and 19-metre "long jumps" ([van Marum et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19173)).

### Ear, words and the advice people will give him

**Known, stage by stage.** Every from-scratch hearing agent feeds a short-time spectrum (FFT, log-mel or MFCC features), not the raw waveform, through a small encoder into the same recurrent core as the other senses. Non-speech sound is learned in 3×10⁷–10⁸ steps, and a raw-waveform ear learns about five times slower and erratically ([Hegde et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02195); [SoundSpaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.11474)). Recognising isolated words needs few weights but many recordings. A network of 9k–100k weights reaches 94–97% on 12 words for unseen speakers, given about 3,000 recordings per word from about 2,600 speakers. Accuracy is still 90% at 125 recordings per word and falls fast below about 50 ([BC-ResNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04140); [Lin et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.01322)). Synthetic speech is a weaker teacher: 56.7% on real speech from 92 synthetic voices, and 92.6% from a voice-cloned set filtered through recognisers ([Quintas et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12745)). Two small agents have tied spoken words to action from scratch. One learned 4 words, with 1,000 recordings each, in 1.5–5×10⁶ steps, reaching 0.87–0.945 success on unheard recordings; without a loss on the sound channel it reached 0.135–0.185 on the harder task ([Chang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09172)). The other followed 6 spoken commands after 10⁹ frames, with some seeds failing ([Hegde et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02195)). At text level, reward alone teaches nothing after millions of episodes, while prediction or reconstruction of the language stream does ([Hermann et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06551)). Curricula lift rule-reading from 23–45% to 65% ([RTFM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08210)). A parent who describes after the fact what the learner did turns failure into about 80% success, works when given for 1% of experience, and keeps 80% of the benefit at 50% noise ([ACTRCE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04546)).

**What published work says about advice in words.** This is the team's open question. Three findings answer most of it. First, **agents use language that merely predicts outcomes only when a world model must predict that language.** In HomeGrid, hints about where objects are, how bins open, and "no, turn around" raised Dynalang's score from about 3.3 to 4.8–6.0 after 50M steps. A model-free R2D2 used only the language correlated with reward, and its "performance drops as it receives more diverse language" (figure reading, 2 seeds; [Dynalang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01399)). Second, **an agent raised only on good teachers cannot doubt a bad one.** The largest social-learning agent "happily follows an incorrect demonstration and even reproduces that incorrect path once the expert has dropped out" ([Cultural transmission 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00715)). Third, **learned trust has only tabular and action-advice precedents.** Advise treats each right-or-wrong label as evidence that is correct with probability C, and with an estimate of C near 0.5 advice is worth nothing; the agent is given C rather than learning it ([Griffith et al. 2013](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2013/file/e034fb6b66aacc1d48f445ddfb08da98-Paper.pdf)). No published deep-RL study tests spoken or written hints that are sometimes false. The design that matches "advised, never commanded" is Dynalang's: words enter as observations, reward comes only from needs, and words matter to the extent that they help predict what happens next (inference). Pretrained models fit around him best on the description side, as narrator, labeller and judge. A noisy labeller (54.7% precision) still lifted naming from 7.1% to 64.4% ([Sumers et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12507)). A reward written by a language model was exploited: the agent "learns to find hallucinogens to dream of the goal state" ([Motif](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00166)).

**Take as published.** Use a fixed log-mel or MFCC front end (40 bins, 25–30 ms window, 10 ms hop), a two- or three-layer encoder, and concatenation into the one recurrent state. Put a loss on the sound channel itself; in a world model that means predicting next-step audio features. Words enter as one unit per step, predicted like any other sense. Augment with ±100 ms time shift and background noise at probability 0.8, use many voices, and filter synthetic clips through a recogniser. A pretrained parent outside him narrates through text-to-speech, asynchronously and cached ([ONI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23022)). Staging: eye first, the ear by prediction before words are paired, frequent isolated words first, speaking last. Do not take pretrained embeddings or speech features inside him. All of T5, BERT, SentenceBERT, wav2vec and HuBERT appear inside the learners of the papers above.

**Reproduce first.** Speech Commands with a speaker-disjoint split: DS-CNN with 24K weights at 94.4%, or BC-ResNet-1 with 9.2K weights at 96.6–96.9% ([Hello Edge](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07128); [BC-ResNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04140)). The data-budget curve: 97.4% with all recordings, 94.8% at 1,000 per word, 90.3% at 125 ([Lin et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.01322)). Each takes one CPU session. Chang's 4-word task: 0.945 with the sound-label loss, 0.870 without on the arm task, and 0.920 against 0.135 on navigation. Advise in 5×5 Pac-Man at three teacher settings. BabyAI GoToRedBallGrey in 21 ± 5k episodes ([BabyAI 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12770)).

## Order, cost and timeline

The evidence reorders the plan in one important way. The world's economy and the harness come before any brain work, because both can void every later result at no cost to fix. It also moves brain A onto the critical path, because its reproduction and its stage 2 runs both need the T4. The table is a schedule built from the notes' costs. The A arm sets the date of the stage 2 verdict: roughly mid-January to February 2027 on the free T4 alone (inference). Four levers shorten it, each with a stated price. Lower T to 500,000 decisions for all arms, halving A's cost at the price of censoring slower learners. Run A at a ratio of 256–512 if the ratio experiment shows little loss. Use DayDreamer's free-running learner and record the ratio it achieves. Or move A to the Mac mini once its speed is known. Shrinking A's run count instead throws away the large-effect sensitivity that justified the comparison. The rule from the methods literature applies: if the budget cannot support a correct experiment, run a different one, and run fewer arms rather than fewer runs ([Patterson et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01315)).

Some things should stop. No more short races: the record predicted every null result so far. No rerun of the 57M control-path contest except as the notes' clean version, on DeepMind Control humanoid-walk with a pass mark of 600 so the answer is checkable against published scores. No more humanoid walking attempts in a single world with plain learners; use the reflex-controller recipe on the T4 or Yu's CPU recipe. No more world-rule races; use the published rules plus the test card. No more word-matching retrieval in the diary; key it on learned state and read nearest neighbours.

## Conclusion

The new evidence moves the bottleneck. The first review read the race failure as a setup problem inside the learners. The deeper problem sits one level up: a world whose easy food cannot sustain life, and a toolchain whose own error rates were never checked. Both are cheap to fix this week, and both would have voided a perfect brain comparison. The second shift is in cost. A, the brain the record favours for the long run, is also the one free hardware can least afford. The plan's honest timeline for "choose the brain" is set by T4 hours, not by kernel sessions. That favours a stage 2 that keeps 20 runs per arm, accepts a "not distinguished" verdict as a decision rule, and lets B start stage 3's cheap reproductions while A finishes.

What remains genuinely unanswered is narrower and more interesting than the first review suggested. Raw-speech grounding and memory across episodes both exist in print at small scale. What does not exist is a creature that dies and is reborn in one persistent world, measured for what it kept, learning from words that people say to him. Some of those words are true, some false, and he must work out whom to believe. Those experiments, together with the need-driven chain of fire, cooking and shelter, are where the team's own compute should go once the instrument is proven.
